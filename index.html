01 · The problem

You fix the devices.
The tracking breaks you.

If you run a repair counter in Karol Bagh or a mobile shop in Chennai, nobody handed you a workshop management system. Your system is a stack of devices, a paper job book, and your own memory — and all three quietly fall apart on a busy day.

📞

"Bhaiya, is my phone ready?"

The same customer calls three times a day. You put the phone down, walk to the bench, check, walk back — for a job you haven't started yet. Every day, ten times over.

📓

The job book is a guessing game

Which device is whose? What was the reported fault? What did you quote? It's all in a diary in your handwriting, and half the entries have no status at all.

🔩

Parts and labour never add up

You put in a Rs 3,200 display and forgot to write it down. At pickup you're quoting from memory, eating the margin, or arguing over a number you can't back up.

🛡️

Warranty is "trust me"

A customer says the fault came back within warranty. You have no record of when you delivered it or what you did — so you redo it for free, or lose a regular over an argument.

02 · The insight

A repair isn't a mystery — it's a known pipeline. Every device moves through the same stages, yet the shop still tracks each one in its head.

MarammatBook's whole job is to make that pipeline visible: intake the job once, then move it received → diagnosing → awaiting-parts → repairing → ready → delivered — so a glance at the board answers "is it ready?" before the customer even asks, and a warranty is a lookup, not an argument.

03 · How it works

Intake once. Then just move it along.

  1. 1

    Intake the job

    Customer, phone, device, IMEI or serial, the reported fault and your estimate. One short form and the job card is on the board — takes a minute at the counter.

  2. 2

    Move it through the pipeline

    Received → diagnosing → awaiting-parts → repairing → ready → delivered. Advance the status with a tap; the board always shows exactly where every device is.

  3. 3

    Log parts & labour as you go

    Add each part with its cost and set the labour. MarammatBook totals the bill for you — no memory maths, no margin quietly leaking away.

  4. 4

    Ready → WhatsApp, deliver → warranty

    Mark it ready and a "come pick it up" message drafts itself. On delivery, set the warranty-until date — and every past repair becomes a searchable warranty record.

07 · Who it's for & questions

Built for the repair counter, not the enterprise service centre.

MarammatBook is for the independent repair shop — a mobile counter, a laptop-and-electronics workshop, a two-technician bench. Busy enough that jobs get lost and "is it ready?" eats your day; small enough that a heavyweight service-desk suite is overkill. If you're running it on a paper job book right now, this is for you.

Mobile repair shops Laptop & PC repair Electronics & appliance 1–4 technicians
Does it actually message the customer, or just remind me to?

MarammatBook drafts the "ready for pickup" message — with the customer's name, device and job number — and stages it in your outbox the moment you mark a job ready. You send it from your own number with a tap, so it looks like it came from your shop, not a robot.

How does the warranty check work?

When you deliver a repair you set a warranty-until date. Later, a customer walks in claiming the same fault came back — you search their phone number or IMEI and MarammatBook instantly shows whether that repair is still in warranty and what was done. No paper slips to dig for.

My shop takes phones, laptops, TVs, mixers — anything. Does that fit?

Yes. A job is just a device, an IMEI or serial, a reported fault and an estimate — so it works for any mobile or electronics repair. The status flow and parts-and-labour log are the same whether it's a screen replacement or a board-level repair.

I'm not very techy. How long to get going?

You can intake your first job in a minute — customer name, phone, device, fault, estimate. The status board and warranty lookup come pre-built. Most shops are running it the same afternoon they start.

What does it cost?

Pricing is worked out on a quick discovery call and billed monthly on subscription. We're keeping public prices off the page while we onboard our first shops.
